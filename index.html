Fit

Worth being honest about.

This is a significant annual commitment and it is not right for everyone. We would rather tell you that on the first call than in the second year.

It earns its fee when
  • Your income is high enough that timing and structure move real money
  • You own a business, or several, and the entity questions are live
  • Equity compensation, real estate or investments make the year complicated
  • You operate or earn in more than one state
  • A sale, a purchase or a liquidity event is somewhere on the horizon

  • A straightforward return is genuinely all you need — take a filing package

Plain English

Six things worth understanding.

Written the way we would explain them on the phone, because that is where most of these questions actually get asked.

Notices

A CP2000 is not a bill

It is a proposed change — the IRS matched a form it received against your return and found a gap. You normally have 30 days to agree or disagree. A surprising share are wrong, or right about the income and wrong about the basis. Never pay one before someone reads it.

Estimates

Why you owe every April

Nothing withholds tax from self-employment, investment or rental income. The fix is quarterly estimates. Pay the lesser of 90% of this year’s tax or 100% of last year’s — 110% if last year’s AGI topped $150,000 — and the underpayment penalty goes away.

Entity

When an S-corp starts to pay

The saving is on self-employment tax, and it only exists once profit comfortably exceeds a reasonable salary for your role. Below that, the payroll and filing costs eat it. The salary is not optional and not a number you pick — it has to be defensible.

Records

How long to keep things

Three years is the general rule. Six if you omitted more than 25% of your gross income. Employment tax records, four. Property records until three years after you sell it. If you never filed, or filed fraudulently, there is no limit at all.

Multi-state

Where you actually owe

Your resident state taxes everything; other states tax what you earned there, and your home state usually credits you for it. Remote work, a rental elsewhere, or a mid-year move each create a return most people miss — and states are getting better at noticing.

Extensions

An extension buys time to file

Not time to pay. File one and you avoid the failure-to-file penalty, which is ten times heavier than the failure-to-pay penalty. But interest and the smaller penalty still run from the original date on anything unpaid.

None of this is complicated. It’s just unfamiliar, and it arrives when you’re busy.

Before you file

What to have ready.

The portal gives you a checklist built for your situation. This is the short version, so you can start gathering before you book anything.

Everyone
  • Last year’s filed return
  • Photo ID and Social Security numbers for everyone on the return
  • W-2s, and 1099s of every letter — NEC, MISC, INT, DIV, B, R, G, K
  • 1095-A if you had marketplace health coverage
  • 1098 for mortgage interest, 1098-T for tuition, 1098-E for student loan interest
  • Bank routing and account number for the refund or payment
  • Any IRS or state letters received since your last filing
Self-employed and business owners
  • Profit and loss and balance sheet, or the bookkeeping file
  • Bank and credit card statements for every business account
  • Mileage log, or total business and total miles for the year
  • Home office square footage and total home square footage
  • Asset purchases over $2,500 with dates and amounts
  • Payroll reports and copies of 1099s you issued
  • K-1s from any partnership or S-corp you hold
  • Estimated tax payments made, with dates
